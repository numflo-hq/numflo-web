# German style guide (de)

Rules every German string must follow (SDLC §4.4, step 2).

1. **Variant:** standard German (Hochdeutsch) for Germany, Austria and Switzerland. Avoid words used
   in only one country; where the term differs, explain it once (e.g. "Grundsteuer (in Österreich:
   Grundsteuer, in der Schweiz: Liegenschaftssteuer)" only where it matters).
2. **Address the reader as "du"**, friendly and clear, as modern finance apps do ("Berechne deine
   Rate"). Capitalise "du/dein" only at the start of a sentence.
3. **Numbers in running text:** full stop for thousands, comma for decimals, a space before % and
   before the currency code: `250.000`, `2.013,98`, `7,5 %`. Four-digit numbers also take the full
   stop (`1.500`). Numbers shown by the calculator are formatted automatically.
4. **Quotation marks:** „German quotes" („…“). **Dashes:** en dash with spaces ( – ).
5. **Capitalisation:** nouns are capitalised as German requires; headings use sentence case otherwise
   ("Wie Zinseszinsen funktionieren").
6. **Compounds:** join compound nouns (Zinseszinsrechner, Kreditkartenschulden); use a hyphen only
   with abbreviations or for readability (CAGR-Rechner, Tilgungs­plan is written Tilgungsplan).
7. **Length:** page titles ≤ 60 characters, meta descriptions 70–155 (checked automatically).
   German runs about 30 % longer than English: prefer short words and active verbs.
8. **Meaning over words:** translate what the English means, never word by word. The independent
   back-translation review must give English with the same meaning as the source.
9. **No legal promises:** results are "Schätzungen" and "keine Finanzberatung".
